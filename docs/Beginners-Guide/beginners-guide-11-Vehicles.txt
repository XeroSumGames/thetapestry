====================================================================
 11. VEHICLES
====================================================================

A vehicle is a multi-seat token that the party drives between campaign-map pins, garages in tactical scenes, brews in, and fights from. Vehicles have fuel, cargo, slots for crew, and a sheet of their own.


WHERE THEY LIVE
---------------
Open the Vehicle dropdown in the Table header to see the campaign's vehicles, or click the vehicle bubble that floats on the campaign map at the party's position.

Each vehicle card has a Popout button (purple) that opens the vehicle sheet in a 900x700 separate window. Dock it on a second monitor and leave it open.


SEAT ASSIGNMENT
---------------
A vehicle has named slots:

  DRIVER      Required to move. Rolls Drive checks for travel and combat.
  NAVIGATOR   The shotgun seat. Rolls NAVIGATE checks for routing, hazard-spotting, and brewing support.
  BREWER      Optional crafting station. See Brewing below.
  PASSENGERS  Up to six.
  SHOOTERS    If the vehicle has mounted weapons, each gets its own slot.

Each slot has a dropdown of eligible characters. Picking a candidate stages the assignment - it doesn't commit yet. Click "MOVE HERE" to confirm: the character's token walks to the slot's cell and auto-snaps to it. Already-aboard characters can reassign freely without the move step.

CLOSE-RANGE GATE
  Candidates more than 30 feet from the vehicle (edge to edge) are greyed out in the dropdown with a "too far" tooltip. They have to walk closer first.

DISEMBARKING
  Every occupied slot has a Disembark button. One click ejects the character to the first free cell in an expanding ring around the vehicle. No range check needed.

PASSENGERS IN MOTION
  When the vehicle is moving, passengers vanish from the tactical map - they're inside the vehicle, not standing on the grid. A green count badge on the vehicle token shows how many are aboard.


FUEL
----
Every vehicle has a fuel tank. Driving burns fuel; running out strands you.

Fuel storage works via INSTALLABLE 55-GALLON DRUMS. The sheet has a "Fuel Reserves" section with Install and Uninstall buttons. Each installed drum adds one day of fuel storage to the vehicle. Drums live in the vehicle's cargo until installed; you can ferry them between vehicles, refill them at fuel pins on the world map, or scavenge for more.

The vehicle sheet shows current fuel as a bar with explicit gallons remaining.


BREWING
-------
Some vehicles support BREWING - a way to convert raw materials into alcohol, biodiesel, or other consumables while you drive. Requires a Brewer in the brewer slot, brewing supplies in the vehicle's stockpile, and the action.

Per vehicle the sheet tracks brewing_supplies_current against a cap (Minnie's cap is 2, for example).

  GATHER MATERIALS  Appears when supplies are below cap. The action pre-stages raw materials and rolls a check; outcome determines yield.
  BREW              Consumes 1 supply unit and rolls the brewing check.

Brewing fires its own narrative banner in the roll feed (BREW prefix) with six outcome variants.


CARGO
-----
Each vehicle has cargo capacity. Cargo is shared - any campaign member can edit a vehicle's inventory. Items move between PC inventory and vehicle cargo freely when the PC is at the vehicle.


THE TACTICAL MAP
----------------
A vehicle in a tactical scene shows as a multi-cell object token. Driver, Navigator, Passenger, and Shooter slots are anchored to specific cells of the token. Open the vehicle popout from the token to see and reassign seats.

Vehicles can take damage. A vehicle's WP and RP track like a character's - they have their own Lasting Wounds chip when seriously damaged.

A ranged attack against a target standing behind a vehicle takes a defense bonus automatically (Chapter 8).


COMBAT
------
The Driver can declare Vehicle Attack actions: ramming, swerving, drive-bys. Each fires its own narrative banner (DRIVE prefix). Navigators can declare NAVIGATE actions to spot hazards or set up the Driver's roll.

Passengers and Shooters can take normal actions out the vehicle - shooting, throwing, etc. - within their weapon range from the vehicle's footprint.
